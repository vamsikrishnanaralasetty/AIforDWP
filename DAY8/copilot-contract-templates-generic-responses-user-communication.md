## Why does Copilot give generic answers?

This is one of the most important things to understand about how Copilot works, so we want to explain it clearly.

Copilot has two sources of knowledge it can draw on:

1. **Your organisation's own documents** — files stored in SharePoint, OneDrive, Teams, and your email. Copilot can read and reason over these if it can find them.

2. **Its own built-in general knowledge** — a broad understanding of common topics, including general legal and commercial concepts, built in during its training.

**When Copilot can find your specific documents**, it uses them to give you precise, grounded answers based on your actual content.

**When Copilot cannot find your documents** — because they are not yet searchable, or because of how the question is asked — it does not produce an error. Instead, it falls back to its general knowledge and gives you a broadly accurate but generic answer. This is what appears to be happening in your case.

This is not a fault with Copilot — it is a sign that something is preventing it from connecting to your contract templates library.

---

## What we think is happening

There are a few common reasons why Copilot might not be connecting to your templates library:

- **The library may not yet be fully set up for search** — Microsoft 365 needs to catalogue your documents before Copilot can find them. If the library was created or updated recently, this can take up to 72 hours to complete.
- **Access permissions** — Copilot can only use documents you are authorised to access. If permissions on the library are set up in a way that limits your access, Copilot will not be able to read the files.
- **Document security settings** — Some documents carry security markings that restrict how they can be processed. If your templates have these applied, it may prevent Copilot from reading them.

We are currently checking each of these possibilities for your library.

## What you can do right now — tips for better Copilot results

While we investigate, there are a few things you can try that often produce much better results:

**1. Be specific about the document**  
Instead of asking: *"What does our template say about indemnity clauses?"*  
Try: *"Summarise the indemnity clause in [document name]"*  
Using the document's actual name helps Copilot find and focus on the right file.

**2. Share the document link with Copilot**  
In Copilot Chat, you can paste a direct link to a SharePoint document. This tells Copilot exactly which file to read, rather than asking it to search for one.

**3. Open the document first, then ask Copilot**  
If you open a contract template in Word and then use Copilot within Word (via the Copilot button in the toolbar), it will automatically work with the document you have open — no searching required.

**4. Add context in your question**  
The more specific your question, the better the answer. For example: *"In our standard services agreement template, what is the liability cap clause, and does it include consequential losses?"*

These approaches can make a significant difference while we work on the underlying issue.
