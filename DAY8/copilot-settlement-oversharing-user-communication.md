## What we believe happened — and what it does not mean

We want to be straightforward with you: **Copilot cannot access documents that you do not already have permission to see.** It works within the same security system as the rest of Microsoft 365 — it can only use content that your account is authorised to access.

This means that if Copilot showed you this document, your account does have some form of access to it. This does not mean anything improper has occurred. In our experience, the most common explanation is one of the following:

- Your account may be a member of a team or group that was given broad access to the site when it was first set up, perhaps covering all partners or the whole legal team, even for matters you are not directly working on.
- You may have been involved in the matter at an earlier stage — for example, during conflict checking or initial intake — and access was never removed when your involvement ended.

Copilot's ability to search across all of your accessible content can make previously unnoticed access visible for the first time. This is not a fault in Copilot; it is how the system is designed to work. However, it can surface permissions configurations that were not intended to be as broad as they turned out to be.

**We are not assuming that anything has gone wrong until we have completed our checks.** But we take this seriously and are acting promptly.

---

## What we are doing right now

Our team is carrying out the following steps:

1. **Reviewing the permissions on the matter site and folder** — We are checking exactly how your account has access to that document, whether through direct permissions, group membership, or inherited access from the site.

2. **Assessing the scope** — We are checking whether other colleagues may have the same access path, so we can understand the full picture.

3. **Checking whether the document carries the right security markings** — Sensitive legal documents should have appropriate labels applied to control who can access them. We are confirming this is in place.

4. **Reviewing relevant access logs** — Where audit records are available, we are reviewing them to understand the history of access to this document.

We have also informed the relevant internal teams so that appropriate oversight is in place while the review is underway.

A document that might have sat in an unvisited folder for years can suddenly appear in a Copilot summary. This is actually a valuable signal — it helps organisations identify and tighten permissions that may have drifted over time.
